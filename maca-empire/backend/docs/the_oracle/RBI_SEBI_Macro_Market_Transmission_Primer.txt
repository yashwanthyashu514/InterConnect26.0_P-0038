RBI / SEBI Macro-to-Market Transmission Primer — The Oracle

RBI policy rate changes:
- Affects funding costs, bank lending rates, bond yields, and risk-free discount rates.
- Higher rates typically tighten liquidity, compress equity valuations, and strengthen currency (context dependent).

Liquidity and forex:
- RBI liquidity operations (OMO, VRR, CRR) influence short-term rates and currency stability.
- Forex intervention impacts INR volatility and import/export competitiveness.

Equity impact channels:
1) Discount rate channel (valuation multiples)
2) Earnings channel (borrowers’ interest expense, demand)
3) Risk premium channel (global risk-on/off)
4) Sector rotation (financials vs rate-sensitive sectors)

Bond yield curve:
- A steepening curve can signal inflation expectations or growth; flattening can signal slowdown risk.
- Policy expectations drive short end; fiscal supply and term premium drive long end.

SEBI market structure:
- Circuit breakers and margin rules impact volatility propagation.
- Market microstructure (F&O changes, position limits) can impact liquidity/impact costs.

Practical interpretation:
- Always interpret policy changes with inflation trajectory, fiscal stance, global dollar liquidity, and risk appetite.
